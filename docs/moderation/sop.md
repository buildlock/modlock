# Moderation standard operating procedure

Status: operational draft; counsel/operator approval required  
Last reviewed: 2026-09-01

## Intake classes

- Malware/security.
- Competitive advantage/cheat behavior.
- Copyright/trademark or creator ownership.
- Impersonation/false verification.
- NSFW/illegal/harassment/privacy content.
- Misleading metadata/deceptive download.
- General quality/compatibility, which is not necessarily a policy violation.

## Triage

1. Preserve report, target versions/hashes, public presentation, source, reporter category, and timestamp.
2. Remove secrets/private personal information from normal case views; restrict legal/security evidence.
3. Assign severity and immediate-containment need.
4. Check conflicts, prior decisions, repeat behavior, and policy version.
5. Assign a reviewer without a creator/conflict-of-interest relationship.

## Actions

`no-action`, `request-information`, `label/warn`, `age-restrict`, `delist`, `quarantine`, `revoke-version`, `suspend-upload`, `suspend-account`, `emergency-block`, or `refer-to-source/legal/security`.

Every action records policy code/version, exact target, evidence refs, reason suitable for the affected party, reviewer, timestamp, review/expiry, and appeal path. Never silently modify a creator's immutable bytes or verification record.

## Emergency action

Credible malware, active exploitation, or severe unfair-advantage content may be blocked before creator response. A second reviewer examines the action promptly. Restoration requires new evidence/bytes/version; the original record remains.

## Appeals

Appeals are reviewed by someone other than the original decision-maker when feasible. The appellant sees the actionable reason and may provide rights, technical, identity, or context evidence. Final decision and policy interpretation are recorded consistently.

## Quality controls

Weekly sample review for consistency; monthly policy-code and false-positive review during beta; privileged access review; metrics on queue age, action/reversal rates, urgent response, and creator communication—not reviewer speed alone.
